Nineteen years on lip filling lines has taught us that the applicator, tube neck and bulk cannot be signed off separately. A wand gloss usually runs at 1,200–2,500 mPa·s at 25 °C. Below 1,200 mPa·s, the bulk tends to run off the applicator before it reaches the lip; above 2,500 mPa·s, it may stop flowing back into the tube, leaving a hollow channel around the wand. A doe-foot gives roughly 300 mPa·s more operating headroom than a brush, although the actual result still depends on the wiper opening. An anhydrous gloss is generally filled at 70–75 °C at the nozzle. The tube needs controlled pre-warming because an unheated component can make the first 200 units skin over, changing both appearance and fill consistency before the line reaches steady state. Fill control is set at ±3% for a 6 ml gloss, with the declared net content protected at the lower tolerance boundary. In practical terms, the nominal filling target must sit above 6 ml rather than treating 6 ml as the centre regardless of variation. mlmlip checks the initial line setting and conducts interval weight checks during production under the [manufacturing process](/process).

Shade approval is handled under D65 lighting at Delta E <= 0.5. The limit between later production batches widens to Delta E <= 1.0 because pigment lots and processing conditions introduce small shifts even when the master standard remains unchanged. A factory that promises visually identical colour without a measurement method is usually promising more than its instruments can verify.

Pearlescent systems and high-tint shades can shift under filling heat. We have seen a bench-approved berry colour read 2 shades deeper after hot filling, especially where the bulk was judged in a glass sample jar rather than the final PETG tube. For that reason, final approval should use the intended pack, applicator and filling temperature. Pigment dispersion creates a slower failure. In an under-milled anhydrous base, settlement can appear after 10–14 days as a visible gradient inside the tube, even though the fresh bench sample looked uniform. Vanilla and peppermint flavour oils introduce a different compatibility risk: some TPE applicators become sticky after 8–10 weeks because the flavour oil plasticises the material. The issue appears during a stability pull, not during the first sample review.

The standard stability programme runs for 12 weeks at 45 °C and includes 3 freeze-thaw cycles between -10 °C and 25 °C, with each stage held for 24 hours. Thermal shock adds 24 hours at 50 °C followed by 24 hours at -5 °C, repeated for 3 cycles. The inspection focuses on leakage and label lift, while colour shift and applicator condition are recorded against the approved control.

Each production batch is checked for Pb, Cd, Hg and As. Microbial controls cover total plate count and yeast with mould, while S. aureus and P. aeruginosa must be absent. ISO 11930 challenge testing applies to water-containing formulas rather than fully anhydrous gloss, which is one genuine reason a new brand may choose an anhydrous first launch. ISO 22716 covers the manufacturing plant. It does not certify an individual product, and it should not be presented as proof that a particular gloss formula meets every EU or US obligation. For a qualified commercial inquiry, mlmlip can provide MSDS and COA documentation. Relevant test reports are also supplied according to the formula and destination market. For regulatory compliance and packaging requirements in this category, consult our whitepaper on [Lip Gloss Manufacturer with In-Stock Packaging Components](/blog/lip-gloss-manufacturer-with-in-stock-packaging-components/).

For the European Union, CPSR Part A contains the formula safety assessment, while Part B records the GMP-related conclusion and assessor sign-off. Part A alone is not enough to place a product on the EU market. The EU Responsible Person files the CPNP notification before market placement; the portal filing itself is free, but preparation of the CPSR commonly costs about EUR 400–900 per formula.

US MoCRA requires facility registration and product listing. It also requires a responsible person to be identified on the label. These duties are different from the EU Responsible Person arrangement, even where the same brand sells a 6 ml gloss in both regions. Claims and label text should therefore be reviewed for each destination rather than copied from one market file.

COSMOS and ECOCERT concern the formula and raw-material chain, including a minimum of 95% ingredients of natural origin plus processing restrictions for the relevant certification route. They do not certify a plant merely because the plant holds ISO 22716. An ISO certificate is not an eco certificate.

The word “organic” is not defined as a regulated cosmetic claim under EU Regulation (EC) No 1223/2009 in the same way that a COSMOS audit assesses sourcing and processing. A legally reviewed label claim and successful certification audit are separate tests. Buyers should decide early whether they need a cosmetic positioning statement or formal COSMOS certification because the second route can narrow the available ingredient set.

Recent September 2026 discussion data still puts gloss ahead of stain, with 12 tracked gloss topics against 7 stain topics. That signal supports small shade launches, but it does not justify skipping wear and pack checks. Consumer comments about sticky skin-feel are often traced to the formula-applicator relationship, while requests for moisturising skin-feel are better supported through cosmetic conditioning and barrier hydration language than through medical wording.

A lip gloss OEM factory with low minimum order quantity is most useful when the 500-piece run behaves like production rather than a hand-filled presentation sample. The bulk should pass through the intended nozzle, the final wiper should meter the wand, and the approved shade should be checked after the formula reaches its normal fill condition. Otherwise, the pilot confirms only that 500 units can be assembled, not that the product can be repeated.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Q: Can I order 500 pieces split across several shades?

The 500-piece MOQ applies per shade because each colour requires its own bulk preparation, filling setup and line clearance. A mixed-shade trial order can combine 2 or more 500-piece lots under one project, normally using the same stock tube and decoration method. This keeps the trial economical without pretending that 500 total pieces can be divided into many very small filling runs.

### Q: How quickly can mlmlip make a lip gloss sample?

A shade selected from an existing mlmlip colour card can usually be prepared in 3 working days. A new shade or adjusted formula normally takes 7 working days from an approved brief, and the sample fee is refunded against the commercial order. Additional rounds may be needed if a pearlescent or high-tint system changes after a 70–75 °C fill simulation.

### Q: Does a 500-piece pilot include fully custom packaging?

Usually not. A 500-piece pilot is a filling MOQ, while custom components commonly begin at 3,000–5,000 units. The pilot therefore runs in a stock 5 ml, 6 ml, 4.5 ml or 8 ml pack, with decoration selected according to supplier minimums. Buyers can review available components through the [customization options](/customization) before approving the commercial brief.

### Q: What colour tolerance applies to approval and repeat production?

mlmlip uses Delta E <= 0.5 under D65 for initial shade approval. The tolerance between separate production batches is Delta E <= 1.0, since raw-material lots and filling conditions can introduce minor movement. The master shade, filled pack and measurement record should remain together so a repeat order 6 months later is compared with the approved standard rather than a phone image.

### Q: Which compliance files are available for EU and US launches?

For an EU launch, the working file may include CPSR support data and CPNP notification information for the appointed Responsible Person. For a US launch, mlmlip supports the formula and manufacturing data needed for MoCRA product listing, while the brand remains responsible for its market role and label review. MSDS and COA files are available on qualified commercial inquiry, with applicable batch test reports supplied for the agreed 6 ml or other finished format.
