CPNP is the European Commission’s free notification portal, and the EU Responsible Person files the notification before a cosmetic product is placed on the market. The portal fee is EUR 0. The real cost sits in formula review, toxicological assessment, document correction, and Responsible Person coordination; for a conventional lip formula, CPSR work usually falls around EUR 400–900 per formula, subject to ingredient complexity and the assessor’s scope. The Responsible Person remains legally accountable for the CPNP submission. A contract manufacturer should not blur that line.

ISO 22716 also needs careful wording. It covers the manufacturing plant and its cosmetic GMP controls; it does not certify an individual lip gloss, lip oil, or lipstick. Under the usual CPSR structure requested by buyers, Part A contains formula and safety information, while Part B records the assessment conclusion and supporting GMP position. Part A alone will not place a product legally on the EU market.

For United States distribution, MoCRA follows a separate path. It calls for facility registration and product listing. A responsible person must also be identified on the label, so an EU notification package cannot simply be renamed and used as the complete US file. A 6 ml wand gloss is usually developed between 1,200 and 2,500 mPa·s at 25 °C. Below 1,200 mPa·s, the bulk tends to run off the applicator; above 2,500 mPa·s, it may stop flowing back into the tube after use. A doe-foot provides roughly 300 mPa·s more operating headroom than a brush, although the exact result depends on stem clearance and wiper geometry.

An anhydrous gloss normally reaches the nozzle at 70–75 °C. The PETG tube must be pre-warmed, or the first 200 units can develop a surface skin before the filling temperature settles. On a 6 ml gloss, our normal fill-weight tolerance is plus or minus 3%, with the declared net content set so the lower tolerance boundary still holds the stated amount. Shade approval is measured rather than judged only by phone photographs. The approval target is Delta E <= 0.5 under D65 illumination, while the production-to-production limit widens to Delta E <= 1.0. Pearlescent and high-tint systems can shift under filling heat, and we have seen a bench-approved colour read 2 shades deeper after hot filling.

Under-milled pigment dispersion in an anhydrous base may appear acceptable during the first inspection. After 10–14 days, settling can show as a colour gradient inside the clear tube. That is why colour sign-off and stability timing cannot be compressed into the same 24-hour review.

Flavour oils create a different compatibility risk. Vanilla and peppermint oils can plasticise certain TPE applicators over 8–10 weeks, producing a sticky tip during the stability pull even though the original bench sample looked clean. For this reason, the pack named in the CPSR support file should be the same material combination used for compatibility testing.

COSMOS and ECOCERT assess the formula and raw-material chain rather than treating a factory certificate as an eco certificate. Their common framework expects at least 95% ingredients of natural origin, together with defined processing restrictions. ISO 22716 cannot be presented as a substitute for that review.

The word “organic” is not itself a specifically regulated cosmetic claim under EU Regulation (EC) No 1223/2009. A legal label assessment and a COSMOS audit are therefore 2 different tests. Buyers planning natural-origin positioning should declare that intention before sampling, because replacing a raw material after CPSR completion may require a revised formula assessment.

Testing normally includes 12 weeks of accelerated stability at 45 °C and 3 freeze-thaw cycles between -10 °C and 25 °C, with each stage held for 24 hours. Thermal shock adds 24 hours at 50 °C followed by 24 hours at -5 °C, repeated for 3 cycles while the team checks leakage and label lift.

Each commercial batch is reviewed for Pb, Cd, Hg, and As. Microbial work covers TPC plus yeast and mould, with absence testing for S. aureus and P. aeruginosa. ISO 11930 challenge testing applies only to formulas containing water, which is one practical reason many first launches begin with an anhydrous lip oil or gloss.

MSDS, COA, and applicable test reports are provided on qualified commercial inquiry. These records support the Responsible Person’s assessment, but no isolated certificate replaces the complete Product Information File or CPNP notification. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### Q: Who actually files the CPNP notification for an EU lip product?

The EU Responsible Person files through the European Commission portal before the product is placed on the market. Beautychain Limited can prepare the formula records, test package, pack photographs, and label information, then coordinate the filing workflow on behalf of the buyer. The CPNP portal costs EUR 0, while CPSR work commonly costs EUR 400–900 per formula.

### Q: Can a 500-piece lip gloss pilot use fully custom packaging?

Usually not, because a 500-piece quantity is our filling MOQ while custom components often begin at 3,000–5,000 units. A pilot generally uses a stock 5 ml or 6 ml PETG tube, or another validated library pack. Custom decoration may still be possible, depending on the process and supplier minimum.

### Q: How quickly can mlmlip produce a shade sample?

An existing colour-card shade can usually be sampled in 3 working days. A new shade from an approved brief normally takes 7 working days, and the sample fee is refunded against the resulting order. Shade approval targets Delta E <= 0.5 under D65, while separate production batches are controlled to Delta E <= 1.0.

### Q: Does ISO 22716 replace the CPSR or CPNP process?

No. ISO 22716 covers the factory’s cosmetic GMP system, while the CPSR addresses the formula’s safety file and CPNP records the product notification. Under EU Regulation (EC) No 1223/2009, these documents serve different purposes, and a plant certificate alone cannot place a product on the EU market.

### Q: Is an anhydrous lip formula easier to launch in the EU?

In most cases, an anhydrous formula removes the need for an ISO 11930 challenge test because that test applies to water-containing products. The product still needs stability work, compatibility review, CPSR completion, and CPNP notification. It must also pass batch controls for 4 named heavy metals and the required microbial parameters.
