EUR 400–900 is a normal working range for the safety assessment of one lip formula, yet buyers still lose weeks because they treat the CPSR as a certificate that can be requested after production. It is not. A cosmetic product safety report for lip care products depends on the final formula, raw-material records, exposure assumptions, packaging contact details, test evidence, and an appropriately qualified expert’s sign-off. Change the fragrance oil or pigment loading after review, and the assessment may need to be reopened before the EU Responsible Person can proceed. The 100-piece flexible pilot batch, 7-day sampling, and fee-credit terms behind this production route are summarised on [Custom Lip Gloss Tube Packaging: Acrylic vs. Glass vs. Custom Molded Components (2026 Buyer's Guide)](/blog/lip-gloss-packaging-tube-guide/).

EU Regulation (EC) No 1223/2009 separates the CPSR into Part A and Part B. Part A contains the formula safety assessment and supporting data, while Part B records the safety conclusion, reasoning, warnings, assessor credentials, and approval. Plant GMP evidence remains a separate part of the product information file. Beautychain Limited coordinates CPSR preparation and expert sign-off for qualified commercial projects, rather than handing the buyer a formula sheet and expecting an outside assessor to reconstruct 19 years of manufacturing records.

ISO 22716 applies to the manufacturing plant. It does not certify an individual 6 ml gloss, and it does not replace the CPSR. We have seen this go wrong when an ISO certificate is inserted into a product file as if it established formula safety; an EU Responsible Person will usually ask for the toxicological profile, impurity data, exposure calculation, test results, and assessor qualifications instead. The same distinction applies to [custom lip products](/products) sold under a buyer’s own trademark.

CPNP notification is filed by the EU Responsible Person before a product is placed on the EU market. The portal filing itself is free. Most of the EUR 400–900 cost sits in technical review and CPSR preparation, although unusual ingredients or missing supplier files can move the fee higher. A finished CPSR also does not complete the CPNP step automatically, because the Responsible Person still needs the label, pack image, notification data, and market-facing product identity.

The US route is different. MoCRA requires facility registration and product listing, while the label must identify the responsible person and provide the required contact route. A CPSR prepared for Europe can support an internal compliance file, but it does not replace MoCRA obligations. We normally map the EU and US document streams during [formula customization](/customization), before a buyer commits artwork to 3,000–5,000 components.

Shade approval is another place where safety documentation and production reality meet. Our approval target is Delta E <= 0.5 under D65, while the permitted difference between production batches widens to Delta E <= 1.0. Pearlescent systems and formulas with a strong tint load can read as much as 2 shades deeper after hot filling, so the approved reference should be a filled production-equivalent unit rather than a cold bench drawdown. Before you commit to a full production run, it is worth checking how [Private Label Lip Gloss Manufacturer FDA MoCRA 2024 Compliant USA](/blog/private-label-lip-gloss-manufacturer-fda-mocra-2024-compliant-usa/) structures sampling and shade matching.

An under-milled pigment dispersion in an anhydrous base may settle within 10–14 days and produce a visible gradient in the tube. The first sample can look acceptable. The stability pull does not. Vanilla or peppermint flavour oils can also plasticise certain TPE applicators over 8–10 weeks, appearing as a sticky doe-foot long after the buyer has approved the initial appearance.

For a 6 ml wand gloss, we usually work between 1,200 and 2,500 mPa·s at 25 °C. Below 1,200 mPa·s, the bulk runs from the applicator and can collect around the neck. Above 2,500 mPa·s, it may not flow back into the tube, leaving voids that buyers sometimes mistake for underfilling. A doe-foot gives roughly 300 mPa·s more working headroom than a brush, though the exact result depends on stem clearance and wiper geometry.

Anhydrous gloss is generally filled at 70–75 °C at the nozzle. The PETG tube has to be pre-warmed within its validated handling window, or the first 200 units can skin over before the line reaches steady state. Fill control is +/-3% on a 6 ml gloss, with the declared net content still holding at the lower tolerance boundary. That last point matters more than the nominal machine setting.

Our stock library includes 5 ml and 6 ml round PETG tubes with screw caps, a 4.5 ml slim tube, an 8 ml squeeze tube with a slant tip, and a 3.5 ml ABS stick. PCR PETG is available at 30% and 50%. Clarity drops visibly at 50%, which is not necessarily a defect, but it should be accepted against a physical sample rather than a digital rendering.

A refillable aluminium shell uses a PP inner cup, keeping the aluminium away from direct formula contact. Bamboo-clad ABS caps require a separate supplier window of 4–5 weeks and are the most common cause of a missed launch date in our experience. Buyers often approve the cap appearance first, though the closure torque and formula compatibility are closer to the critical path. Accelerated stability normally runs for 12 weeks at 45 °C, accompanied by 3 freeze-thaw cycles between -10 °C and 25 °C, with each stage held for 24 hours. Thermal shock uses 24 hours at 50 °C followed by 24 hours at -5 °C for 3 cycles. The inspection looks for leakage and label lift, as well as formula separation and applicator change.

Each production batch is checked for Pb, Cd, Hg, and As against the applicable market limits. Microbial review covers total plate count, yeast and mould, plus absence of S. aureus and P. aeruginosa. ISO 11930 challenge testing applies to formulas containing water; it is usually not required for a properly substantiated anhydrous formula, which is one practical reason a new brand may launch an anhydrous lip oil or gloss first.

COSMOS and ECOCERT assess the formula and its raw-material chain rather than merely approving the factory. A qualifying route generally expects at least 95% ingredients of natural origin, together with processing restrictions and traceability evidence. An ISO 22716 plant certificate is not an environmental or natural-origin certificate and should not be presented as one.

The word “organic” is not independently regulated as a cosmetic claim under EU Regulation (EC) No 1223/2009 in the same way that the product safety framework is regulated. A defensible label statement and a COSMOS audit therefore answer different questions. The first is reviewed against evidence and consumer interpretation; the second follows a certification standard with formula and supply-chain criteria.

Documentation such as MSDS, COA, and relevant test reports is provided on qualified commercial inquiry. For an EU launch, the working file should also connect the exact INCI formula to raw-material specifications, impurity information, packaging contact data, stability evidence, and the signed CPSR version. Version control matters. A lipstick assessed at version 2.1 should not enter CPNP under an unreviewed version 2.3.

This does not mean every 500-piece pilot has a completed market file before colour approval; it means the intended EU, UK, or US route is identified early enough that the sample does not depend on an ingredient or claim that later blocks expert approval. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### Q: Who can sign a CPSR for an EU lip product?

Under EU Regulation (EC) No 1223/2009, Part B must be completed and signed by a suitably qualified safety assessor with the required university-level credentials in pharmacy, toxicology, medicine, or a comparable discipline recognised by an EU member state. Beautychain Limited coordinates the formula dossier, evidence review, and expert sign-off, but the named assessor remains professionally responsible for the safety conclusion.

### Q: How long does a cosmetic product safety report for lip care products take?

A complete, conventional formula can often be assessed within 7–15 working days after all data are available. Missing toxicology records, unclear fragrance declarations, or incomplete impurity specifications can extend that window. CPSR preparation should begin before final artwork because warnings and use instructions identified in Part B may need to appear on the label.

### Q: Does a 500-piece pilot include custom packaging?

Usually not. The 500-piece level is a per-shade filling MOQ, while printed or moulded components generally begin at 3,000–5,000 units. A pilot therefore uses a stock 5 ml, 6 ml, or another compatible library pack, allowing the buyer to review filled colour, applicator behaviour, and transport condition before committing to dedicated packaging.

### Q: Can one CPSR cover several lip shades?

Sometimes, but not automatically. An assessor may accept a shade range under 1 report where the base remains fixed and the colourants are covered through a documented worst-case approach. A high pigment load or a materially different flavour system may require separate exposure reasoning, additional evidence, or another CPSR. The final decision belongs to the qualified assessor.

### Q: Is ISO 22716 enough to sell a lip gloss in Europe?

No. ISO 22716 demonstrates that the plant operates under cosmetic GMP principles, but it does not replace the product information file, the signed CPSR, or CPNP notification by the EU Responsible Person. The label must also comply with EU requirements before the product is placed on the market. For US distribution, MoCRA facility registration and product listing remain separate obligations.
