## Rheology, Fill Temperature, and Why the First 200 Units Matter

A stick or pot format built on beeswax behaves differently from a wand gloss, but the fill physics share one problem: temperature control at the nozzle. Our anhydrous beeswax balm formulas fill at 70–75 °C, and if the receiving component is not pre-warmed, the first 200 units skin over — a thin solidified film forms before the bulk of the fill settles, trapping a discontinuity that shows up as a wrinkled surface under inspection. We pre-warm all components as a standard step on the line, not an option, because we have seen what happens when it is treated as optional.

Viscosity targets for a lip balm in stick format are different from gloss rheology, but the principle of specifying a number rather than a descriptor still applies. A beeswax-dominant anhydrous base at 25 °C will typically read between 1,800 and 2,400 mPa·s in its molten state just above the pour point — specific enough to set a process parameter, vague enough to need a formula-by-formula confirmation. We run rheology on every new formula before we commit a fill parameter to the batch record, because a ceramide-loaded balm and a plain beeswax-castor balm with the same declared drop point can behave differently at the nozzle. To align packaging compatibility and batch compliance, consult our [custom lip gloss manufacturer](/lip-gloss-manufacturer/) production overview.

Colour development in a beeswax base carries one consistent risk: pigment dispersion in an under-milled anhydrous base settles in 10–14 days and shows up as a gradient in the stick or pot. We mill to a fineness that prevents visible settling over the 12-week accelerated stability window, and shade approval is held to Delta E ≤ 0.5 under D65 illuminant at the point of approval. Between production batches, that tolerance widens to Delta E ≤ 1.0, which is the practical limit of what a skilled colourist can hold across different raw material lots. Buyers who have worked with other [customization](/customization) houses sometimes come in expecting tighter inter-batch tolerances; in our experience, Delta E ≤ 1.0 batch-to-batch is achievable and Delta E ≤ 0.5 batch-to-batch is not sustainable at commercial scale without a colour-matching overhead that adds cost most buyers do not want to pay.

Fill weight tolerance on a 6 ml gloss runs +/−3% with the declared net content held at the lower bound. The same +/−3% applies to a 4 g balm stick — the declared weight is not an average, it is a floor, and every unit that falls below it is a non-conformance. We pull fill-weight checks at the start, middle, and end of every run, not just at the start.

## Stability Testing and Compliance Documentation

Twelve weeks of accelerated aging at 45 °C, combined with three freeze-thaw cycles at −10 °C and +25 °C for 24 hours each, is the baseline stability protocol for every formula we release. For products going into EU or US trade, we add a thermal shock sequence: 24 hours at 50 °C followed by 24 hours at −5 °C, repeated three cycles, watching specifically for leakage and label lift. An anhydrous beeswax formula will usually pass the thermal shock without leakage, though we have seen edge cases where a high-fragrance-load formula softened the TPE applicator tip enough to deform it — that failure mode appears in the stability pull, not in the bench sample, which is one reason we do not shortcut the full protocol.

Heavy metals — lead, cadmium, mercury, arsenic — and microbial limits (total plate count, yeast and mould, absence of Staphylococcus aureus and Pseudomonas aeruginosa) are tested per batch. Because our beeswax balm formulas are anhydrous, ISO 11930 preservative efficacy challenge testing does not apply, which removes one document from the compliance stack and is a real, practical reason to launch anhydrous before adding a water phase. Before you commit to a full production run, it is worth checking how [CPNP Notification Support for Private Label Lip Products](/blog/cpnp-notification-support-for-private-label-lip-products/) structures sampling and shade matching.

On the compliance documentation side, ISO 22716 covers the plant and its GMP processes — it does not certify the product. A buyer placing product on the EU market needs a CPSR with both Part A (the formula safety assessment, prepared by a qualified assessor) and Part B (the GMP statement from the plant). Part A alone will not get a product onto the EU market. CPNP notification is filed by the EU Responsible Person before the product is placed on the market; the portal itself is free, so the cost sits in the CPSR, which in our experience runs EUR 400–900 per formula depending on the assessor and the formula complexity. US MoCRA requires facility registration, product listing, and a responsible person named on the label — three separate actions, not one. For seasonal launches and trend-driven drops, [Private Label Lip Gloss Manufacturer FDA MoCRA 2024 Compliant USA](/blog/private-label-lip-gloss-manufacturer-fda-mocra-2024-compliant-usa/) covers the lead times and flexibility you will need to plan around.

COSMOS and ECOCERT certification applies to the formula and the raw material chain, specifically a minimum of 95% ingredients of natural origin plus processing restrictions. A plant ISO certificate is not an eco certificate. We are direct about this because we have seen it misrepresented, and a buyer who lists "COSMOS certified" on packaging without a valid formula-level certificate is taking a legal risk, not a marketing one. "Organic" is also not a regulated claim under EU Regulation (EC) No 1223/2009, which means a legal label claim and a COSMOS audit are two different tests with two different outcomes.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Q: What does "certified organic beeswax" actually mean in a contract manufacturing context?

It means the beeswax in the formula has been sourced through a supply chain audited to an organic standard — in practice, COSMOS or an equivalent national organic certification — and that the raw material traceability is documented to the supplier level, not just to the COA on the drum. At mlmlip, the organic beeswax matrix in our lip balm formulas carries that chain documentation, which is what a COSMOS-certified formula assessor will ask to see during Part A of the CPSR. A manufacturer who says "we use organic beeswax" without that chain documentation is describing a marketing claim, not a certified input.

### Q: How do COSMOS certification and EU CPNP notification relate to each other — are they the same process?

They are separate processes with different owners. COSMOS certification is a formula and raw-material-chain audit conducted by an accredited certification body; it confirms that the formula meets the COSMOS standard for natural and organic cosmetics. CPNP notification is an administrative filing made by the EU Responsible Person on the EU portal before the product is placed on the EU market — it is free to file, but it requires a completed CPSR (both Part A and Part B) before the Responsible Person will sign off. A product can be CPNP-notified without being COSMOS certified, and a formula can be COSMOS certified without being CPNP-notified. Most buyers sourcing an [organic beeswax lip balm contract manufacturer](/products) for the EU market need both, but they are on different timelines and different budgets.

### Q: What happens to the beeswax matrix during a −10 °C to 45 °C shipping cycle?

A well-specified beeswax matrix at 68–74 °C drop point will survive that cycle without bloom or structural failure in most cases. The risk is not melting — 45 °C is well below the drop point — but rather the repeated contraction and expansion of the fill inside the component, which can open a hairline gap between the fill and the tube wall in a stick format. We test for this specifically with three freeze-thaw cycles at −10 °C and +25 °C, 24 hours each, and with the thermal shock protocol. Components with a poor fit between the fill diameter and the tube bore are more susceptible; we check dimensional compatibility before the first fill run.

### Q: What documentation do I need for US MoCRA compliance, and can you provide it?

MoCRA requires facility registration (which covers our plant), product listing filed by the responsible person named on the label, and that responsible person's contact information on the label itself. We provide the plant's facility registration number, ISO 22716 GMP statement, COA, and MSDS on qualified commercial inquiry. The product listing is filed by the brand owner or their designated US responsible person, not by the contract manufacturer — that distinction matters because the brand owner holds the legal responsibility for the listing's accuracy.
