10 ppm can decide whether a lip gloss shipment clears a buyer’s review or sits in quarantine while the brand searches for raw material records. The number is often treated as a universal lead limit. It is not. The United States, European Union, Canada, and individual retailers apply different legal reasoning, guidance, impurity expectations, and documentation rules, so a passing laboratory result still needs to be read against the destination market. Brands running this format for the first time usually start from the capacity and MOQ benchmarks laid out in [Private Label Lip Gloss Manufacturer FDA MoCRA 2024 Compliant USA](/blog/private-label-lip-gloss-manufacturer-fda-mocra-2024-compliant-usa/).

For lip cosmetics heavy metals testing requirements for exporters, the practical starting point is a full-panel report covering Pb, Cd, Hg, and As for each commercial batch. At Beautychain Limited, that report is tied to the batch number rather than reused as a general statement about the formula. ICP-MS is usually the preferred analytical method because it reaches suitable reporting limits across all 4 elements, though the laboratory’s sample digestion method matters with wax-rich lipstick and heavily pigmented lip mud.

A result sheet without the laboratory name, test method, detection limits, sample identification, and authorised sign-off is weak evidence. We have seen this go wrong when a brand received a one-page “pass” certificate that could not be traced to either its shade or filling batch. A complete heavy metals test report closes that gap, provided the shade code and lot number on the report match the goods being exported.

The US FDA has issued guidance recommending a maximum lead level of 10 ppm in externally applied cosmetics, including lip products, but that does not turn every other element into an identical 10 ppm specification. Color additives also have their own identity and impurity requirements. “export compliant” should not appear in a cosmetic sales file, since finished lip cosmetics do not receive that type of general premarket approval.

EU Regulation (EC) No 1223/2009 approaches heavy metals through prohibited substances and technically unavoidable traces under Article 17, where the trace must remain consistent with good manufacturing practice and product safety. Most buyers assume the EU provides one simple finished-product table for Pb, Cd, Hg, and As, though it is closer to a safety-assessment decision supported by raw material controls and analytical evidence. The CPSR assessor may therefore request lower reporting limits or pigment-specific information even when another customer accepted the same batch.

Canada adds another reference point. Health Canada’s guidance commonly used by importers identifies impurity limits of 10 ppm for lead, 3 ppm for arsenic, 3 ppm for cadmium, and 1 ppm for mercury. A North American launch covering both Canada and the United States should be specified against the stricter applicable value rather than tested once and interpreted later.

The source is often the colour system, not the filling line. Mineral-derived pigments can carry trace Pb or As from their feedstock, while a low-cost pigment dispersion may introduce variation between lots. Our incoming review checks the pigment COA and supplier specification before shade work starts. Finished-product testing then verifies the actual commercial combination because a compliant base does not prove that every deep berry or brown shade will produce the same result.

Shade development introduces another control point. Pearlescent and high-tint systems can shift under fill heat, and we have seen a shade approved on a bench sample read 2 shades deeper after hot filling. An under-milled anhydrous pigment dispersion may settle in 10–14 days, leaving a visible gradient in the tube even though the day-1 sample looked uniform. The [custom lip product range](/products) should therefore be assessed as a filled pack, not only as formula held in a laboratory jar. The 100-piece flexible pilot batch, 7-day sampling, and fee-credit terms behind this production route are summarised on [Custom Lip Gloss Tube Packaging: Acrylic vs. Glass vs. Custom Molded Components (2026 Buyer's Guide)](/blog/lip-gloss-packaging-tube-guide/).

Approval colour is measured at Delta E <= 0.5 under D65. Between production batches, the working tolerance widens to Delta E <= 1.0 because pigment lots and thermal history introduce normal variation. A visual approval remains useful, but instrument readings make the approved standard reproducible after the original sample has aged for 6 months.

Rheology affects both appearance and dosing. A wand gloss usually works between 1,200 and 2,500 mPa·s at 25 °C. Below 1,200 mPa·s, the formula tends to run off the applicator; above 2,500 mPa·s, it may stop flowing back into the tube and leave an apparent void around the wiper. A doe-foot generally buys about 300 mPa·s of headroom over a brush.

Anhydrous gloss is normally filled at 70–75 °C at the nozzle. The tube needs pre-warming because the first 200 units can skin over when cold components pull heat from the formula. That early skin formation creates variable fills and marks around the neck, which is why line clearance includes both temperature records and retained units from the start of the run.

On a 6 ml gloss, our normal fill-weight control is +/-3%, with the declared net content still holding at the lower bound. This means the nominal machine setting cannot merely equal the label declaration. Product density and line variation have to be included in the target setting, or the lightest acceptable unit may fall below the declared content.

Packaging can alter both stability and the test plan. The standard [packaging customization options](/customization) include 5 ml and 6 ml round PETG tubes with screw caps, a 4.5 ml slim tube, an 8 ml squeeze tube with a slant tip, and a 3.5 ml ABS stick. PCR PETG is available at 30% or 50%, although clarity drops visibly at 50% and can change how pale shades appear at retail.

A refillable aluminium shell uses a PP inner cup, so the aluminium never contacts the formula. Bamboo-clad ABS caps require a separate supplier lead time of 4–5 weeks and remain the most common cause of a slipped launch date in our experience. A 500-piece filling pilot does not remove that component constraint.

Flavour oils also require pack-specific observation. Vanilla and peppermint oils can plasticise some TPE applicators over 8–10 weeks, showing up as a sticky tip during a stability pull rather than in the initial bench sample. Our standard stability programme runs for 12 weeks at 45 °C and includes 3 freeze-thaw cycles between -10 °C and 25 °C, with each exposure held for 24 hours. Thermal shock uses 24 hours at 50 °C followed by 24 hours at -5 °C for 3 cycles. Leakage and label lift are recorded alongside formula separation and applicator changes.

Microbial testing is completed per batch with TPC, yeast and mould, plus absence testing for S. aureus and P. aeruginosa. ISO 11930 challenge testing applies only to formulas containing water. That is one genuine reason many first-time exporters launch an anhydrous gloss or lip oil before developing a water-containing lip mask.

ISO 22716 covers the manufacturing plant and its cosmetic GMP system; it does not certify an individual product. COSMOS and ECOCERT address the formula and raw material chain, including a minimum of 95% ingredients of natural origin and processing restrictions. A plant ISO certificate is not an eco certificate and should never be represented as one.

The word “organic” is not independently regulated as a cosmetic claim by EU Regulation (EC) No 1223/2009. A legally supportable label statement and a COSMOS audit are therefore 2 separate tests. The supporting file should match the exact formula placed on the market rather than a similar stock base.

EU compliance also separates CPSR Part A from Part B. Part A contains the cosmetic product safety information and assessment inputs, while Part B records the safety assessor’s conclusion and associated GMP statement; Part A by itself will not place the product legally on the EU market. CPNP notification must be filed by the EU Responsible Person before sale.

The CPNP portal is free, although preparing the CPSR generally costs about EUR 400–900 per formula. That cost belongs in the launch budget before the shade range expands. A 6-shade line may share substantial base-formula information, but the safety assessor decides whether shade-specific data or separate assessments are necessary.

US MoCRA requires facility registration, product listing, and a responsible person identified on the label. The responsible person also needs an adverse event process and access to adequate safety substantiation. Heavy metal results support that file, but they do not replace registration or listing.

Sampling begins within 7 working days after an approved brief, or about 3 working days when the buyer selects an existing colour card. The sample fee is refunded against the commercial order. Buyers can submit formula, shade, destination, and pack information through the [sample request desk](/samples), which reduces repeated questions during laboratory review.

The pilot MOQ is 500 pieces per shade, and that is a filling MOQ rather than a packaging MOQ. Custom components commonly begin at 3,000–5,000 units, so a 500-piece pilot generally uses stock packs. We prefer to state that distinction before artwork starts because a pilot quantity cannot usually support a new mould or a custom bamboo-clad cap.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Q: Does every lip cosmetic batch need heavy metals testing?

Beautychain Limited tests Pb, Cd, Hg, and As per commercial batch because colour materials and their trace impurity profiles can vary between lots. Some markets do not phrase this as an explicit universal batch-testing command, but exporters still need evidence that technically unavoidable traces remain safe and controlled. A batch-linked result is stronger than a formula-level declaration created 12 months earlier.

### Q: Is a passing heavy metals report enough for EU or US sale?

No. The EU also requires a completed CPSR, an EU Responsible Person, compliant labelling, and CPNP notification before the product is placed on the market. US MoCRA requires facility registration and product listing, with the responsible person named on the label. The [export planning brief](/quote) should identify both the destination and sales channel because retailers can impose limits beyond baseline law or guidance.

### Q: Can a 500-piece pilot use fully custom packaging?

Usually not. The 500-piece pilot is a filling MOQ, while packaging suppliers commonly require 3,000–5,000 units. The pilot normally uses a stock 5 ml, 6 ml, 4.5 ml, 8 ml, or 3.5 ml format so the buyer can assess colour, dispensing, leakage, and consumer handling before committing to a larger component order.

### Q: Why test each shade if the base formula is unchanged?

Pigment loading can differ materially between a clear gloss, a deep berry shade, and an opaque brown. Those pigments may bring different trace levels of Pb, Cd, Hg, or As, while fill heat can deepen certain shades by as much as 2 visual steps. Lip cosmetics heavy metals testing requirements for exporters are therefore best applied to the actual shade and production lot rather than inferred from the clear base.

### Q: How quickly can an export-ready sample be prepared?

An existing colour card can normally be sampled in 3 working days, while a custom approved brief requires about 7 working days. Commercial release takes longer because 12-week accelerated stability, 3 freeze-thaw cycles, thermal shock, compatibility review, and destination documents cannot be compressed into a bench-sample schedule.
