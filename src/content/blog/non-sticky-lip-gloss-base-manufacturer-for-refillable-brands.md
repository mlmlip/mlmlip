An anhydrous gloss entering a cold tube starts setting at the wall, while the centre remains mobile, so the surface defect is often blamed on the formula even though the package temperature caused it. For a refillable brand, that early error is more expensive because the insert, shell, and extraction geometry all have to remain compatible after repeated handling. For a wand gloss, our usual working range is 1,200–2,500 mPa·s at 25 °C. Below 1,200 mPa·s, the product tends to run off the applicator and may collect around the neck. Above 2,500 mPa·s, it does not flow back into the tube quickly enough, leaving visible voids after use. A doe-foot typically provides about 300 mPa·s more operating headroom than a brush because it carries and releases the base differently. That allowance is useful, though it does not replace a complete package compatibility pull.

Anhydrous gloss normally reaches the filling nozzle at 70–75 °C. The tube or refill cup must be pre-warmed, otherwise the first 200 units can skin over before the line reaches thermal balance. Our [development process](/process) records nozzle temperature, component temperature, line speed, and actual fill weight so that a successful laboratory texture can be repeated on production equipment. “Non-sticky” remains a sensory target rather than a single instrument reading; drag, stringing, cushion, and after-feel are assessed alongside the 25 °C viscosity result.

Fill control matters just as much in a refill. On a 6 ml gloss, the normal filling tolerance is ±3%, but the declared net content must still hold at the lower production bound. In practice, the machine target is set above 6 ml so that the lowest conforming unit does not fall below the label declaration. The same discipline applies to colour: Delta E `<= 0.5` under D65 is the approval limit, while Delta E `<= 1.0` is the working limit between accepted production batches. Colour can change during filling. Pearlescent and high-tint systems may read 2 shades deeper after hot filling than they did in a bench sample, which is why approval should include a filled-package standard rather than a jar drawdown alone. An under-milled pigment dispersion in an anhydrous base can settle after 10–14 days and show a visible gradient through the tube. We check that interval before treating a fresh sample as stable. Vanilla and peppermint flavour oils can plasticise some TPE applicators over 8–10 weeks. The first sign is usually a sticky tip in the stability pull, not a problem in the initial bench sample. A refill system adds another interface, so MLMLIP tests the formula against the applicator and the PP cup rather than issuing a general compatibility statement for all TPE grades.

The standard stability programme runs for 12 weeks at 45 °C and includes 3 freeze-thaw cycles between -10 °C and 25 °C, with each stage held for 24 hours. Thermal shock uses 24 hours at 50 °C followed by 24 hours at -5 °C for 3 cycles, with leakage and label lift recorded at each inspection. These checks do not simulate every warehouse or consumer condition, though they reveal many closure and adhesive failures before shipment.

Each production batch is screened for Pb, Cd, Hg, and As. Microbial review covers TPC and yeast-and-mould counts, while S. aureus and P. aeruginosa must be absent. ISO 11930 challenge testing applies only to water-containing formulas, which is one practical reason many new brands launch with an anhydrous gloss first. On a qualified commercial inquiry, MLMLIP provides relevant MSDS, COA, and test reports rather than making an unsupported compliance claim.

ISO 22716 covers the manufacturing plant. It does not certify an individual gloss, its environmental positioning, or the final refill assembly. For the European Union, CPSR Part A is the formula safety assessment and CPSR Part B contains the plant GMP statement; Part A by itself will not place the product legally on the EU market. These are separate documents even when a service provider delivers them together.

CPNP notification is filed by the EU Responsible Person before the product is placed on the market. Filing through the CPNP portal is free, so the material cost normally sits in CPSR preparation, which is commonly about EUR 400–900 per formula. In the United States, MoCRA requires facility registration and product listing. It also requires the responsible person to be identified on the label, subject to the applicable company role and product particulars.

COSMOS and ECOCERT concern the formula and raw-material chain rather than the ISO 22716 status of the plant. Their framework generally requires at least 95% ingredients of natural origin together with processing restrictions. A plant ISO certificate is not an environmental certificate and should never be presented as one. “Organic” is not a regulated cosmetic claim under EU Regulation (EC) No 1223/2009, so label legality and a COSMOS audit remain 2 different tests.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Q: Can MLMLIP make a 500-piece refillable lip gloss pilot?

Yes, MLMLIP can fill 500 pieces per shade as a pilot, provided a compatible stock insert and outer pack are available. The 500-piece quantity is a filling MOQ, not a custom packaging MOQ. If the selected refill shell starts at 3,000–5,000 units, the brand can either buy the component quantity or run the pilot in a stock pack before committing to the shell.

### Q: How is a non-sticky claim checked at 25 °C?

MLMLIP checks instrument viscosity together with panel observations at 25 °C because tack is not represented by one number. A wand gloss usually sits between 1,200 and 2,500 mPa·s, while the panel checks drag and stringing, followed by cushion and moisturising skin-feel. The claim should remain cosmetic and should not imply treatment of a lip condition.

### Q: Is a refillable aluminium shell compatible with every gloss formula?

No. The aluminium should remain isolated from the formula by a PP inner cup, and the cup must be tested with the base over the 12-week stability programme. Flavour oil, closure pressure, and exposure at 45 °C can change the result. MLMLIP normally approves the actual production component rather than relying on a shell drawing or a general resin declaration.

### Q: What colour tolerance applies to the first production order?

The approved shade standard is controlled to Delta E `<= 0.5` under D65, while later production batches are accepted at Delta E `<= 1.0` against the approved reference. For heat-sensitive shades, MLMLIP uses a hot-filled unit as the master because a bench sample may appear up to 2 shades lighter before filling.

### Q: Which documents are needed for EU and US sales?

For the EU, the file normally needs CPSR Part A and Part B, followed by CPNP notification through the EU Responsible Person before sale. Under US MoCRA, the route includes facility registration and product listing, while the label identifies a responsible person. MSDS, COA, and relevant test reports are provided on qualified commercial inquiry, but none of those documents should be described as an “export compliant” product status.
