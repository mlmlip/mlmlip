Buyers asking about usp 51 and usp 61 testing for lip cosmetics are usually trying to answer 2 different questions. USP <51> assesses whether the preservative system can control specified microorganisms after deliberate inoculation, while USP <61> measures the number of viable aerobic microorganisms present under defined test conditions. Neither chapter, by itself, certifies that a lip cosmetic is compliant in the European Union or the United States.

For a water-containing lip tint or lip mask, USP <51> can be part of a credible preservation program. ISO 11930 is generally the more direct cosmetic reference for a challenge test, particularly for an EU file, and the selected method should be agreed before the laboratory begins. An anhydrous gloss may not require the same challenge protocol because microorganisms need available water to grow, which is one real reason a new brand often launches an anhydrous [lip product range](/products) before attempting an emulsion-based lip mask.

USP <61> is not a substitute for release specifications. Our normal batch review covers total plate count, yeast and mould, plus absence testing for Staphylococcus aureus and Pseudomonas aeruginosa. Heavy-metal screening covers Pb, Cd, Hg, and As per batch. On a qualified commercial inquiry, Beautychain Limited can provide the applicable MSDS, COA, laboratory reports, and a complete antimicrobial effectiveness testing report rather than a one-page pass statement.

The practical distinction becomes important during formula selection. A gloss with no intentionally added water can still receive contamination from raw materials or filling contact surfaces, so routine microbial limits remain relevant even if ISO 11930 is not applicable. A water-containing tint has a different exposure profile. It usually needs a documented challenge study, controlled manufacturing under ISO 22716, and packaging that does not undermine the preservative system over 12 weeks.

ISO 22716 covers the plant and its cosmetic GMP controls; it does not certify an individual formula. We have seen this stated incorrectly on supplier presentations, especially where an ISO certificate is placed beside a product claim without explaining the boundary. The same distinction applies to COSMOS and ECOCERT, which assess the formula and raw-material chain against requirements that include at least 95% ingredients of natural origin and defined processing restrictions. A plant ISO certificate is not an eco certificate. If you are comparing stock versus bespoke options, explore our [lip oil OEM](/lip-oil-manufacturer/) capabilities starting from low-MOQ pilot runs.

The term “organic” is not independently defined as a cosmetic claim by EU Regulation (EC) No 1223/2009. A legal label review and a COSMOS audit are therefore 2 different tests. Buyers developing natural-positioned lip oil should settle the certification route before approving flavours, pigments, or emollients because replacing one non-accepted raw material after testing can restart the challenge study.

On a 6 ml wand gloss, our working fill tolerance is +/-3%, with the declared net content still holding at the lower bound. Shade approval is measured at Delta E <= 0.5 under D65, while the allowable difference between production batches widens to Delta E <= 1.0. Visual review remains useful, though a photograph taken under retail lighting cannot replace a D65 instrument reading.

A typical wand gloss runs at 1,200-2,500 mPa·s at 25 °C. Below 1,200 mPa·s, the bulk tends to run off the applicator; above 2,500 mPa·s, it may stop flowing back into the tube after use. A doe-foot generally provides about 300 mPa·s more working headroom than a brush, but that allowance varies with stem clearance and wiper geometry. Anhydrous gloss commonly fills at 70-75 °C at the nozzle. The tube should be pre-warmed because the first 200 units can skin over when a hot bulk meets a cold component. This defect can trap voids, change the apparent shade, and produce inconsistent fill readings even though the bulk vessel remained within specification.

Pearlescent and high-tint systems also move under fill heat. A colour approved as a bench sample can read 2 shades deeper after hot filling, so our [custom formulation process](/customization) includes a filled-package shade confirmation rather than approval in a glass beaker alone. In an under-milled anhydrous base, pigment dispersion can settle within 10-14 days and appear as a visible gradient in the tube.

Applicator compatibility needs the same patience. Vanilla and peppermint flavour oils can plasticise some TPE tips over 8-10 weeks, creating a sticky applicator that was not present in the first bench sample. Switching the flavour concentration may solve it, though a different TPE grade or applicator material is sometimes required.

The stability plan normally includes 12 weeks at 45 °C and 3 freeze-thaw cycles between -10 °C and 25 °C, with each stage held for 24 hours. Thermal shock adds 24 hours at 50 °C followed by 24 hours at -5 °C for 3 cycles. We watch for leakage and label lift, while also recording shade movement, odour change, and applicator condition. Those studies are packaging-specific; passing in a glass jar does not clear a 6 ml PETG tube.

For EU placement, CPSR Part A and Part B are separate sections with different purposes. Part A records the formula safety information and assessment inputs, while Part B includes the safety assessor’s conclusion and relevant GMP statement. Part A alone will not put a cosmetic product lawfully onto the EU market.

The EU Responsible Person submits the CPNP notification before market placement. CPNP filing itself is free at the portal, so the commercial cost usually sits in CPSR preparation, commonly around EUR 400-900 per formula depending on complexity and assessor scope. Label review, Product Information File maintenance, and local Responsible Person service may be quoted separately.

US MoCRA has another structure. It requires applicable facility registration and product listing. A responsible person must also be named on the label, and serious adverse event records need an assigned handling process. USP methods may support the safety substantiation file, but MoCRA does not turn a USP <51> result into a federal cosmetic approval.

That is why usp 51 and usp 61 testing for lip cosmetics should be written into the development brief as a test scope, not used as a general marketing badge. The report should identify the sample, method, inoculum or enumeration conditions, acceptance criteria, results, laboratory, and dates. If the buyer receives only the word “pass,” the report is difficult to defend during retailer review.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Q: Are USP <51> and USP <61> both mandatory for every lip gloss?

No. USP <51> is an antimicrobial effectiveness test intended for products that rely on a preservative system, while USP <61> is a microbial enumeration method. An anhydrous 6 ml gloss may be assessed through microbial limits, water-activity reasoning, raw-material controls, and stability evidence without a USP <51> study, depending on the market and safety assessor. A water-containing formula is more likely to require ISO 11930 or an agreed equivalent challenge method.

### Q: How long does a complete microbial and stability program take?

Microbial enumeration can often be completed within several working days after laboratory receipt, while a challenge test requires multiple observation points under the selected USP <51> or ISO 11930 protocol. Packaging stability normally runs for 12 weeks at 45 °C, with 3 freeze-thaw cycles and 3 thermal-shock cycles added to the file. A buyer can begin artwork work during testing, but final mass production should not assume a pass before the relevant results are reviewed.

### Q: Can a 500-piece pilot use fully custom packaging?

Usually not without purchasing the supplier’s component MOQ. Our filling MOQ is 500 pieces per shade, whereas decorated tubes, moulded caps, or custom applicators commonly start at 3,000-5,000 units. The practical pilot uses a stock 4.5 ml, 5 ml, 6 ml, or 8 ml pack, allowing the brand to test colour response and moisturising skin-feel before committing to the larger component run.

### Q: Does USP testing replace CPSR or CPNP work for Europe?

No. USP <51> and USP <61> can supply evidence for the safety file, but EU Regulation (EC) No 1223/2009 still requires the appropriate CPSR, Product Information File, label review, and an EU Responsible Person. The Responsible Person files the CPNP notification before placement on the EU market. A typical CPSR costs about EUR 400-900 per formula even though the CPNP portal itself does not charge a filing fee.

For a qualified commercial inquiry, Beautychain Limited can provide relevant MSDS, COA, microbial test reports, heavy-metal results, and the agreed complete antimicrobial effectiveness testing report. The exact file depends on whether the formula is anhydrous or water-containing and whether the destination is the EU or US. ISO 22716 plant documentation may support the file, but it does not replace product-specific test evidence.
