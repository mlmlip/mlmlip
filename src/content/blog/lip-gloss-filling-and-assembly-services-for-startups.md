The sample timetable starts after the brief is approved. A new shade or adjusted base normally takes 7 working days, while an existing colour-card selection can be prepared in 3 working days. The sample fee is refunded against the commercial order, and the [sampling desk](/samples) records the approved reference rather than relying on phone photographs, which are poor colour standards.

Shade approval is measured under D65 lighting at Delta E <= 0.5. Between separate production batches, the working limit widens to Delta E <= 1.0 because pigment lots and bulk processing introduce small variation even under controlled manufacture. Most buyers assume a visual match is enough, though a deep berry shade can look correct in the beaker and read 2 shades deeper after hot filling.

Pearlescent systems and formulas with a heavy tint load are especially sensitive to fill heat. We therefore compare the approved bench sample with the filled component, not only with bulk held in a clear jar. An under-milled anhydrous dispersion may also appear acceptable on day 1 and form a visible gradient after 10–14 days, so an early photograph cannot replace a stability pull.

Wand performance has a narrower operating window than many startup briefs allow. For a typical gloss, we target 1,200–2,500 mPa·s at 25 °C. Below 1,200 mPa·s, the product tends to run off the applicator; above 2,500 mPa·s, it may not flow back into the tube after use. Applicator choice changes that window. A doe-foot generally buys about 300 mPa·s of extra headroom over a brush because it holds more bulk at withdrawal, although the exact result depends on the flock length and wiper bore. That is why our [customization process](/customization) pairs the formula with the applicator before a startup approves decoration.

Flavour oils create a slower packaging risk. Vanilla and peppermint oils can plasticise certain TPE applicators over 8–10 weeks, producing a sticky tip that does not appear during a same-day bench review. Beautychain treats formula-to-pack compatibility as a timed test, not an assembly assumption.

Our standard packaging library includes 5 ml and 6 ml round PETG tubes with screw caps, a 4.5 ml slim tube, an 8 ml squeeze tube with slant tip, and a 3.5 ml ABS stick. PCR PETG is available at 30% or 50%, although clarity drops visibly at 50%. That trade-off should be approved with a physical sample because studio lighting often conceals the grey cast.

A refillable aluminium shell uses a PP inner cup, so the aluminium does not touch the formula. Bamboo-clad ABS caps are also possible, but they carry a separate supplier lead time of 4–5 weeks. In our experience, that cap is the most common reason a launch date slips after the formula has already passed.

Each production batch is checked for Pb, Cd, Hg, and As according to the agreed market specification. Microbial review covers TPC, yeast and mould, plus absence of S. aureus and P. aeruginosa. ISO 11930 challenge testing applies only to water-containing formulas, which is one practical reason many new brands launch an anhydrous gloss before moving into more preservation-sensitive formats.

ISO 22716 certifies the manufacturing controls at the plant; it does not certify an individual gloss. For the EU, CPSR Part A covers the formula safety assessment, while Part B contains the plant GMP statement. Part A alone is not sufficient to place the product on the EU market.

The EU Responsible Person files the CPNP notification before market placement. Filing at the CPNP portal is free, so the material cost generally sits in the CPSR and related assessment work, often around EUR 400–900 per formula. In the US, MoCRA requires facility registration, product listing, and a responsible person identified on the label.

COSMOS and ECOCERT concern the formula and its raw-material chain rather than granting an environmental status to the factory. Their usual framework requires at least 95% ingredients of natural origin alongside processing restrictions. An ISO 22716 plant certificate is not an eco certificate and should never be presented as one.

The word “organic” is not independently regulated as a cosmetic claim under EU Regulation (EC) No 1223/2009. A label claim reviewed for legality and a COSMOS audit are therefore 2 different tests. Buyers should decide which standard they intend to support before approving the INCI composition and artwork.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Q: Can a startup order only 500 lip gloss units?

Yes. Beautychain can fill a 500-piece pilot per shade, but that quantity normally uses a stock 4.5 ml, 5 ml, 6 ml, or 8 ml pack because custom component factories tend to begin at 3,000–5,000 units. The 500-piece quantity is a filling MOQ, which prevents a buyer from mistaking it for a custom packaging MOQ.

### Q: How quickly can the first lip gloss sample be made?

An existing Beautychain colour-card shade can usually be sampled in 3 working days after the brief is confirmed. A new shade or adjusted formula generally requires 7 working days from an approved brief, and the sample fee is refunded against the order. The approved shade is measured under D65 with a target of Delta E <= 0.5.

### Q: What can cause a gloss to leak or feel wrong on the applicator?

At 25 °C, a wand gloss below 1,200 mPa·s may run off the applicator, while a result above 2,500 mPa·s may stop flowing back into the tube. Wiper bore and applicator design also matter; a doe-foot commonly permits about 300 mPa·s more than a brush. Leakage can instead come from cap torque, neck geometry, or thermal cycling, so the filled pack must be tested as a unit.

### Q: Does ISO 22716 make the product compliant for the EU and US?

No. ISO 22716 applies to Beautychain’s plant controls, not to the finished product. EU placement still requires an appropriate CPSR, a Responsible Person, and CPNP notification, while US MoCRA has its own facility registration, product listing, and label responsibilities. The 2 systems overlap in record discipline but are not interchangeable.

### Q: Should a startup choose an anhydrous or water-containing gloss first?

An anhydrous formula is often the simpler first commercial step because ISO 11930 challenge testing applies only to water-containing formulas. It still needs stability work, pack compatibility, and microbial control appropriate to the product. A water-containing option can provide a different moisturising skin-feel, but its preservation system adds development work before a 500-piece launch.
