Flow is set against both the formula and the applicator. A wand gloss normally sits between 1,200 and 2,500 mPa·s at 25 °C. Below 1,200 mPa·s, the formula tends to run off the applicator; above 2,500 mPa·s, it may stop returning cleanly into the tube after use. A doe-foot provides roughly 300 mPa·s more working headroom than a brush, although the exact result depends on stem clearance and wiper geometry.

Packaging compatibility can change a formula that looked stable in a glass laboratory vessel. Vanilla and peppermint flavour oils, for example, can plasticise some TPE applicators after 8–10 weeks. Each batch is checked for Pb, Cd, Hg, and As. Microbial testing covers TPC and yeast and mould counts, while S. aureus and P. aeruginosa must be absent. ISO 11930 challenge testing applies to water-containing formulas; it is generally not required for a properly designed anhydrous gloss, which is one practical reason that an emerging brand may choose an anhydrous first launch.

Documentation such as MSDS and COA files is provided on a qualified commercial inquiry. Relevant test reports are also shared according to the formula, market, and confidentiality status. These records support the responsible person’s product file, but they do not replace the brand’s own adverse-event procedures or US label review.

ISO 22716 covers the manufacturing plant and its cosmetic GMP controls. It does not certify an individual gloss formula. The same boundary applies to eco positioning: COSMOS and ECOCERT assess the formula and raw material chain, including a minimum 95% ingredients of natural origin plus defined processing restrictions; a plant ISO certificate is not an eco certificate and should never be presented as one.

Under EU Regulation (EC) No 1223/2009, “organic” is not itself a regulated cosmetic claim in the same way that a COSMOS audit controls certification eligibility. A legal label assessment and an independent certification audit are therefore 2 different tests. This becomes relevant when a US gloss is expected to enter Europe later with unchanged artwork.

For EU distribution, CPSR Part A contains the formula safety assessment, while Part B records the assessor’s conclusion and GMP-related product safety position. Part A alone will not place a product legally on the EU market. The EU Responsible Person files the CPNP notification before placement on the market; the portal filing is free, but the CPSR commonly costs around EUR 400–900 per formula.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Q: What makes a lip gloss supplier MoCRA compliant for the US market?

A mocra compliant lip gloss manufacturer for us market supply should have the applicable FDA facility registration in place and provide accurate manufacturing information for the responsible person’s product listing. The supplier should also maintain batch traceability, GMP records, and a route for serious adverse-event information. MoCRA support does not mean the factory assumes every responsibility held by the named US responsible person.

### Q: Can we launch a custom shade with only 500 pieces?

Yes, Beautychain Limited can fill a 500-piece pilot per shade in a stock pack. It remains a filling MOQ, so a proprietary tube may require a separate component purchase of 3,000–5,000 units. A shade based on the existing colour card can usually be sampled in 3 working days, while a new approved brief normally requires 7 working days.

### Q: How do you control shade accuracy between samples and production?

The approval target is Delta E ≤ 0.5 under D65 lighting, and the production-to-production limit is Delta E ≤ 1.0. For heat-sensitive colour systems, we compare the filled commercial pack rather than relying only on the cool bench sample. A 10–14 day observation also helps reveal pigment settling that may not appear immediately.

### Q: Does an anhydrous lip gloss need ISO 11930 challenge testing?

ISO 11930 generally applies to formulas containing water and requiring preservative efficacy assessment. A properly designed anhydrous gloss usually follows a different microbiological risk assessment, although each batch still receives the agreed microbial controls. The final decision depends on formula composition, packaging exposure, and the target-market safety assessment.

### Q: Can the same gloss launch in the US and European Union?

It can, but US MoCRA and EU Regulation (EC) No 1223/2009 impose different responsibilities. The US route requires facility registration where applicable, product listing, and a named responsible person on the label. The EU route needs a CPSR with Parts A and B, an EU Responsible Person, and CPNP notification before sale; the typical CPSR cost is around EUR 400–900 per formula.
