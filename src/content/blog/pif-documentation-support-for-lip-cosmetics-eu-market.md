EU Regulation (EC) No 1223/2009 places the file under the control of the EU Responsible Person. The manufacturer supplies much of the technical evidence, but the Responsible Person owns the market obligation and must make the PIF accessible to the competent authority at the address shown on the label. Beautychain Limited supports compilation by aligning formula records, batch tests, manufacturing statements, packaging data, and artwork inputs before the CPSR is released. This is factory-side PIF compilation support, not a replacement for an EU safety assessor or Responsible Person.

Most buyers assume ISO 22716 certifies the finished lip product, though it is closer to evidence that the plant operates under cosmetic GMP. The certificate covers the manufacturing site. It does not approve a 6 ml gloss, validate a moisturising claim, or replace the formula-specific safety review. Under the document structure used for EU launches, CPSR Part A addresses the formula safety information, while Part B records the safety assessment and conclusion; the plant GMP statement remains a separate manufacturing record within the PIF package. Part A alone will not place the product on the EU market.

CPNP notification must be filed by the EU Responsible Person before sale. The portal filing itself is free, so the commercial cost usually sits in formula review, CPSR preparation, label verification, and Responsible Person service. In our experience, a CPSR commonly costs EUR 400–900 per formula, with the final amount affected by fragrance allergens, toxicological data gaps, and the number of shades treated as one product family. A 12-shade line may not require 12 unrelated assessments, but that decision belongs to the qualified assessor after reviewing the common base and pigment differences.

For the United States, MoCRA has a different chain of responsibility. It calls for facility registration, product listing, and a responsible person named on the label, while the EU route uses the CPSR, PIF, CPNP notification, and an EU Responsible Person. A buyer selling on both sides of the Atlantic should not treat one submission as a substitute for the other. The approval standard matters because “match the sample” is not a measurable production instruction. We hold shade approval at Delta E <= 0.5 under D65 illumination, then allow up to Delta E <= 1.0 between commercial batches. Pearlescent and high-tint systems can shift during filling, and a colour accepted in a room-temperature bench cup may read 2 shades deeper after hot filling. We therefore compare the approved drawdown with filled-unit pulls rather than relying only on the kettle sample.

An under-milled anhydrous dispersion can look acceptable on day 1 and settle after 10–14 days, leaving a visible gradient inside the tube. That failure becomes both a consumer issue and a PIF evidence issue because the product no longer matches the stability condition described by the assessor. Vanilla or peppermint flavour oils create another delayed problem: some TPE applicators become sticky after 8–10 weeks because the oil plasticises the tip. A fresh bench sample rarely reveals it.

For a wand gloss, the working range is usually 1,200–2,500 mPa·s at 25 °C. Below 1,200 mPa·s, the bulk runs off the applicator and can collect around the neck. Above 2,500 mPa·s, it may stop flowing back into the tube, leaving an apparent void after use. A doe-foot gives roughly 300 mPa·s more operating headroom than a brush, although the final result still depends on stem clearance and wiper geometry.

Anhydrous gloss normally reaches the filling nozzle at 70–75 °C. The tube must be pre-warmed because the first 200 units can skin over if cold components pull heat from the fill too quickly. Fill weight control is +/-3% on a 6 ml gloss, with the declared net content still holding at the lower tolerance boundary. This sounds like a production detail, but retained fill records and net-content controls support the manufacturing section of the PIF.

Packaging choices also change the evidence package. Our [customization options](/customization) include 5 ml and 6 ml round PETG tubes with screw caps, a 4.5 ml slim tube, an 8 ml squeeze tube with a slant tip, and a 3.5 ml ABS stick. PCR PETG can be specified at 30% or 50%, although clarity drops visibly at 50%. A refillable aluminium shell uses a PP inner cup, so the aluminium does not touch the formula. Brands running this format for the first time usually start from the capacity and MOQ benchmarks laid out in [Private Label Lip Gloss Manufacturer](/lip-gloss-manufacturer/).

Bamboo-clad ABS caps add a separate supplier lead time of 4–5 weeks and are the most common packaging reason we see for a launch date slipping. The PIF should identify the commercial pack assessed for compatibility, especially where a flavour oil contacts a TPE tip or where hot fill can distort a thin-walled tube. Switching components after compatibility work may require a document update and additional stability pulls.

Our usual stability programme runs for 12 weeks at 45 °C and includes 3 freeze-thaw cycles between -10 °C and 25 °C, with each stage held for 24 hours. Thermal shock adds 24 hours at 50 °C followed by 24 hours at -5 °C for 3 cycles. We inspect for leakage and label lift, while also recording colour movement, odour change, applicator condition, and formula separation.

Each commercial batch is tested for Pb, Cd, Hg, and As, together with total plate count, yeast and mould, and absence of S. aureus and P. aeruginosa. ISO 11930 challenge testing applies to formulas containing water. That is one practical reason many new brands launch an anhydrous gloss or oil first: the microbiological risk profile is generally simpler, though routine batch controls still apply. On a qualified commercial inquiry, documentation such as MSDS, COA, and relevant test reports is provided for regulatory review.

COSMOS and ECOCERT apply to the formula and its raw material chain rather than automatically certifying an ISO 22716 plant. A qualifying route typically expects at least 95% ingredients of natural origin as well as restrictions on processing. A factory ISO certificate is not an eco certificate and should not appear in a sales deck as one. “Organic” is not independently defined as a cosmetic claim by EU Regulation (EC) No 1223/2009, so legal label review and a COSMOS audit remain different tests.

A PIF also needs claims to stay proportionate to the evidence. For lip gloss, lip oil, or a 3.5 ml stick, acceptable cosmetic positioning may concern conditioning, barrier hydration, or moisturising skin-feel. It should not imply disease treatment or guaranteed physiological repair. Claim wording is reviewed against the formula, consumer-use directions, and available substantiation before the label is passed to the EU Responsible Person.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Q: What does a lip cosmetic PIF normally contain?

Under Article 11 of EU Regulation (EC) No 1223/2009, the PIF connects the product description, CPSR, manufacturing method with GMP evidence, claim substantiation, and animal-testing information required by the regulation. The Responsible Person keeps the file accessible for 10 years after the last batch is placed on the market. Beautychain Limited prepares factory records and formula inputs, while the appointed EU assessor and Responsible Person complete their regulated functions.

### Q: Can the factory file the CPNP notification for my brand?

The CPNP filing is normally made by the appointed EU Responsible Person before the product enters the market. The portal charge is EUR 0, while CPSR and Responsible Person work creates the actual compliance cost, often beginning around EUR 400–900 per formula for the safety report. We provide the manufacturing evidence and respond to technical questions, but we do not describe factory ISO 22716 status as product approval.

### Q: Is a 500-piece pilot enough for stability and packaging review?

A 500-piece pilot can generate representative filled units for shade confirmation, compatibility observation, and controlled market seeding, provided stock packaging is used. Full accelerated stability still runs for 12 weeks at 45 °C, alongside 3 freeze-thaw cycles and the 3-cycle thermal-shock sequence. A pilot does not shorten those physical test periods, although early pulls can reveal leakage, colour shift, or a sticky TPE tip before mass production.

### Q: Why launch an anhydrous lip gloss before a water-containing formula?

ISO 11930 challenge testing applies to products containing water, while an anhydrous formula usually has a simpler microbiological assessment route. That does not remove batch testing for TPC, yeast and mould, S. aureus, or P. aeruginosa. It can, however, reduce one layer of testing for a first 500-piece launch and make the initial EU dossier easier to coordinate.

### Q: How early should PIF work begin for an EU launch?

PIF documentation support for lip cosmetics eu market entry should begin before the commercial pack is locked, ideally during the first 7-working-day sample cycle. Early review catches ingredient restrictions, allergen declarations, pack-contact concerns, and claim wording before artwork is printed. Waiting until 5,000 decorated units are complete can turn a minor INCI or Responsible Person address correction into a packaging write-off.
